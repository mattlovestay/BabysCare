Emotional Well-being

The famous "development milestones" calendar

This is not a competition, it's a guide to what you can expect.

⚠️ NOTE: Milestones are guidelines, not rules. In this section, you'll find average age ranges. Remember that every baby develops at their own pace. The most important thing isn't when they reach the milestone, but that they maintain consistent progress. If you have any questions, your pediatrician is always your best resource.

Movement And Control

  • 0-3 months: Begins to lift head while on tummy time. Movements cease to be just reflexes and begin to stretch legs.
  • 4-6 months: Rolls over (from back to stomach and vice versa). Begins to sit up with support from hands or cushions.
  • 7-9 months: Able to sit up alone without support. Begins to crawl or creep (be careful !, some babies skip crawling and go straight to trying to stand).
  • 10-12 months: Stands up holding onto furniture (walking phase). Some take their first steps independently.

Learning And Thinking

  • 2-4 months: The baby follows objects with their eyes and begins to recognize people at a distance. They show boredom (cries) if activities don't change.
  • 6 months: Explore objects with your hands and mouth. Begin to transfer objects from one hand to the other.
  • 9 months: Understands the concept of "object permanence" (knows that if you hide a toy under a blanket, the toy is still there). Plays "hide-and-seek" with their hands.
  • 12 months: Explores objects in different ways (shaking, hitting, throwing). Can follow simple instructions such as "give me the toy".

Language and Communication

  • 0-3 months: He is startled by loud noises. He starts making guttural sounds ("ga-ga").
  • 4-6 months: Responds to sounds by making noises. Begins babbling with consonants (ba-ba, ma-ma).
  • 7-9 months: He understands "No." He starts pointing at things to show interest.
  • 12 months: The baby uses simple gestures (shaking their head or waving goodbye). Your baby tries to repeat the words they hear.

Bond And Affection

  • 2 months: The social smile appears (smiling at people on purpose).
  • 6 months: He recognizes familiar faces and is beginning to distinguish strangers. He enjoys playing with others, especially with Mom or Dad.
  • 9 months: Your baby may have "separation anxiety" (cries when mom leaves). They have favorite toys.
  • 12 months: Shows fear in new situations. Extends an arm or leg to help when being dressed.
