Feeding With Love

Health and care in every bite

Breestfeeding

Comfortable breastfeeding requieres a supported back position, with the baby aligned (ear-shoulder-hip) and tummy to tummy, preferably using cushions.

Benefits:

  • Improves milk production
  • Prevents mastitis
  • Fosters confidence

Some tips for a successful lactance:

  • Comfort above all: Find a comfortable place with cushions or pillows to support your back and arms; you'll get tired without them.
  • Bring the baby to your breast: Don't lean towards your baby. Hold them so their face is facing your breast and their nose is level with your nipple.
  • The perfect latch: Make sure your baby takes in most of the areola, not just the nipple; this will help prevent pain.
  • Change positions: Changing posture helps empty different areas of the breast, preventing mastitis
  • Feeding on demand: Don't impose schedules; observe your baby's early hunger signs.
  • Hydration: Drink plenty of water before feeding your baby to stay hydrated. Remember that it's also important to take care of yourself.

Infant Formula

Preparing a baby bottle seems like a simple task, but it has its technique to ensure that the baby receives the necessary nutrients safely.

How to prepare a baby bottle step by step:

  1. Strict hygiene: Wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water. Make sure the bottle, teat, and ring are sterilized (especially during the first few months).
  2. Order matters: First pour the water, then the powder. If you do it the other way around, the water measurement won't be accurate and the mixture could end up too concentrated.
  3. Precise measurement: Always use the measuring spoon that comes in the container. The measurement should be level (you can use the rim of the container or a clean knife to level it). Do not pack the powder or create "mounds".
  4. Standard ratio: Generally, one scoop of powder is used per 30 ml (1 oz) of water. Never change this ratio unless instructed by your pediatrician.
  5. Mix without bubbles: Do not shake the bottle vigorously up and down, as this creates a lot of air (gas for the baby). It is better to roll it between the palms of your hands or gently shake it in a circular motion.
  6. Temperature: Always test the milk on the inside of your wrist. It should be lukewarm, not hot.

Myths and realities

We often let ourselves be influenced by beliefs or things we hear that can either facilitate or affect our confidence about formula milk, so here we debunk the most common ones.

  • Myth: "Formula makes babies sleep longer". Not necessarily. Although formula is slower to digest than breast milk, sleep depends on the baby's neurological development, not just how full their stomach is.
  • Myth: "You always have to boil the water." It depends on the water quality. If you use bottled water with very low mineral content, sometimes it is not necessary to boil it, but it is still the standard recommendation to eliminate any residual bacteria in the powder.
  • Myth: "If I add a little more powder, my baby will grow stronger." FALSE AND DANGEROUS. Excessive powder can cause dehydration or kidney problems in your baby. Always follow the recommended measurements.
  • Myth: "Formula milk causes constipation." It may happen at first, because the digestive system is adjusting, but if the preparation is correct, it shouldn't be a chronic problem.
  • Myth: "You can heat the baby bottle in the microwave." Not recommended. Microwave ovens heat unevenly and create "hot spots" that can burn a baby's mouth and may also degrade some nutrients.

Emotional Bond

Remember that feeding not only nourishes the body, it also strengthens the emotional bond between you and your baby.

Some tips to strengthen the bond even more:

  • Eye contact: When feeding (whether from a bottle or directly at the breast), make sure to avoid distractions. Maintaining eye contact with the baby sends a message of security and complete presence. It's their first love language.
  • Voice and lullabies: Hearing is one of the most developed senses at birth. Speak softly to your baby, sing to them, or simply describe what you are doing. This not only strengthens your bond but also stimulates their future language development.
  • Sensory stimulation: Your baby perceives the smell of the mother's skin, the beat of her heart (which he recognizes from the womb) and body heat, which creates a safe "refuge".
  • Whether you choose or need to feed your baby with the breast or a bottle, the most important thing is that this moment is a space of calm, eye contact, and affection. Milk nourishes their body, but your presence nourishes their soul.
