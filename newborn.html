New Borns

All you need to know

Umbilical Cord

When a baby is born, the umbilical cord is cut, leaving a stump. The stump should dry and fall off when the baby is 5 to 15 days old. Keep the stump clean with gauze and water only. Sponge the rest of your baby as well. Do not put your baby in a tub of water until the stump has fallen off. Let the cord fall off naturally. Do not try to pull it off, even if it is hanging by a thread. Watch the umbilical cord stump for signs of infection. This is not common, but if it occurs, the infection can spread quickly.

Signs of a local infection at the stump include:

  • Yellowish, foul-smelling discharge from the stump
  • Redness, swelling, or tenderness of the skin around the stump
Be aware of the signs of a more serious infection. Contact your baby's healthcare provider immediately if your baby has:
  • Poor feeding
  • Fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher
  • Lethargy
  • Poor, floppy muscle tone
If the umbilical cord stump is pulled off too soon, active bleeding may begin, meaning that every time one drop of blood is wiped away, another appears. If the cord stump continues to bleed, contact your baby's provider right away. Sometimes, instead of drying completely, the cord will form pink scar tissue called a granuloma. This granuloma drains a clear, yellowish fluid. This often goes away in about a week. If it doesn't, contact your baby's provider. If your baby's stump hasn't fallen off within 4 weeks (and most likely much sooner), contact your baby's provider. There may be a problem with your baby's anatomy or immune system.

When to see a Pediatrician

Warning sings:

  • Fever and feeding: Temperature above 38°C (rectal) or if the baby refuses to eat.
  • Behavior: Extreme drowsiness, difficulty walking, or excessive irritability.
  • Physical problems: Yellowing of the skin or eyes, frequent or severe vomiting, diarrhea, dehydration.
  • Respiration: Rapid, noisy breathing, or retraction of the ribs.

First Bond Between mother and baby

The fist mother-baby bond, or attachment, is a crucial emotional and physical connection that begins during pregnancy and is sealed at birth through skin-to-skin contact, eye contact, and breastfeeding. This inicial "fusion" regulates the newborn's heart rate, temperature, and stress levels, establishing the foundation for their emotional security and future neurological development. Important things about the first bond:

  • Pregnancy and initial contact: It begins with the voice and movement felt in the womb, and is consolidated in the "sacred hour after birth, facilitating oxytocin and protection."
  • Skin-to-skin: It's essential to calm the baby, regulate its temperature and promote early breastfeeding.
  • Constant interaction: It's strengthened by responding to crying, looks and physical contact(caressing).
  • Benefits: Promotes physical, mental, emotional, and social development, creating security and confidence
  • IMPORTANT* It's not immediate, so don't worry, It's normal for attachment to be built gradually amd not happen instantly, especially after a difficult birth.
